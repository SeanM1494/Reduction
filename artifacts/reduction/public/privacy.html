Privacy Policy

Effective September 21, 2026

Recipe Reduction (“we”, “us”) turns a recipe into a diagram you can cook from. It is available as an iPhone app and at recipereduction.com. This policy explains what we collect, why, who handles it for us, and what you can do about it. We wrote it to describe what the app actually does, not to cover every possibility.

What we collect

Your account. You sign in with Google or with Apple. From the provider we receive your name, your email address and an identifier for your account with them. If you use Sign in with Apple’s “Hide My Email”, the address we receive is Apple’s relay address, not your real one. We never see your password.

Your recipes and cooking progress. The recipe links you paste, the text you paste, the photos you take of a recipe page, the recipes the app extracts from them, and how you use them: which steps you have completed, your serving size, ratings, meal types, and any timer you set. This is the content of your library and it is stored so it is there on every device you sign in on.

Recipe pictures. When the page a recipe came from has a picture of the dish, we fetch a copy when you save the recipe and store it with it, so your library shows it without your device ever contacting that site. You can also attach a photo of your own to any recipe, replace it, or remove it. These pictures are part of your library: they are stored with it and deleted with it.

Purchases. Subscriptions in the app are sold and billed by Apple through In-App Purchase. Apple tells us that a purchase happened, its transaction identifier, its status and when it renews or ends. We never collect or store your card details; Apple holds those. On the website, subscriptions are billed by Stripe under the same rule: Stripe holds the card, we hold only a subscription identifier and its status.

Notifications. If you turn on timer notifications, we store the token your phone gives us so we can send the notification. Nothing else about your phone is collected for this.

Technical records. Like any web service we keep server logs, which include your IP address and the pages requested, for a short time, to keep the service running and to investigate problems. We also record whether a free recipe was used and whether a request was allowed or refused by the free-recipe limit, so we can see that the limit works. These records are tied to your account identifier, not to your name.

Before you have an account. The first recipe can be diagrammed without signing in. We set a cookie so that recipe can be kept for you if you create an account afterwards.

What we do not collect

No advertising identifiers, no location, no contacts, no analytics profile of you, and no data from other apps. We do not sell personal information and we do not share it for advertising.

How we use it

  • To sign you in and keep your library in sync across your devices.
  • To extract a recipe from a link, text or photo and show it as a diagram.
  • To know whether your account may add another recipe, and to record and honour your subscription.
  • To send you the timer notifications you asked for.
  • To keep the service running, find faults, and prevent abuse.
  • To answer you when you write to us.

Who handles data for us

We use a small number of service providers, each for one job, and each receives only what that job needs:

  • Google and Apple for sign-in.
  • Anthropic for extraction. The recipe link, pasted text or photo you submit is sent to Anthropic’s API to be read into a recipe. A photo of a recipe page is not kept by us after extraction; what we keep is the recipe that came out of it. (A picture you attach to a recipe as its picture is different: that is kept, as described above, and is not sent to Anthropic.)
  • Apple (App Store) and Stripe (website) for subscriptions and billing.
  • Expo for delivering timer notifications to phones.
  • Replit, which hosts the service and its database in the United States.

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect the rights and safety of people using the service.

Cookies and local storage

The website uses a session cookie so you stay signed in, the pre-account cookie described above, and browser storage for your appearance preference. The app stores your sign-in token and preferences on the device. There are no advertising or tracking cookies.

How long we keep it

Your account, library and purchase records are kept for as long as you have an account. Server logs are kept for a short period and then discarded. Recipes extracted from public recipe pages may be kept in a cache that is not linked to any account, so that the same page does not have to be extracted twice.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from Settings, in the app or on the website. Deletion removes your account, your recipes, your cooking progress, your notification tokens and our records of your subscription. Deleting your account also cancels any active subscription we are able to cancel: a website subscription is cancelled immediately. An App Store subscription can only be cancelled by you, in your device’s Settings under your Apple Account, Subscriptions; the app reminds you of this before and after deletion. Deletion cannot be undone.

Security

Data travels over encrypted connections. Sign-in tokens are stored hashed. Access to the database is limited to what the service needs to run. No system is perfectly secure, and if we learn of a breach affecting your data we will tell you.

Children

Recipe Reduction is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 13. If you believe a child has created an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Your rights

You can see and change your recipes and progress in the app at any time, and you can delete everything by deleting your account. Depending on where you live, you may also have rights to ask for a copy of your data, to have it corrected, or to object to certain uses. Write to us at the address below and we will respond. Because the service is hosted in the United States, your data is processed there wherever you use it from.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we will update the date at the top and, for significant changes, tell you in the app or by email.
